# Cardboard Kingdom: art and character bible

*Written for 2.24 (2026-11-01), when Alek asked for characters that are "way cuter and more stylistic, like Costume Quest, Terraria or Stardew Valley, where the characters are meaningful and people can relate and care about them". It pulls together what the game already does, so new characters and places stay consistent. The structure follows the art-bible and narrative templates in the reference repos Alek shared (Claude Code Game Studios, worldcodex). Those were used as checklists. No text or art was copied from them.*

Related: [DESIGN_DIRECTION.md](DESIGN_DIRECTION.md) (the Costume Quest plan), [CHARACTERS.md](CHARACTERS.md) (who everyone is), [STORY.md](STORY.md) (what happens).

## 1. The look in one sentence

**A kingdom a kid built out of boxes, painted by someone who loves them:** round, soft, warm and homemade, cosy in the day and glowing at night, never mean-looking for long.

## 2. Shape language

| Who | Base shape | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Kids (Sam, Jules) | Big round head, tiny body, dot eyes, rosy cheeks | Costume Quest proportions. The face carries every feeling. |
| Villagers (critters) | A ball head on a little cardboard box body, paws holding the box | Everyone in the Kingdom is "wearing" a box. It's the Kingdom's uniform. |
| Monsters (Brinemarsh) | Pear shapes, drips, fins, big eyes | Soft and wobbly. A bit spooky at first, then obviously friendly. |
| Villains (Society) | Normal-looking birds in masks and good coats | The danger looks respectable, which is the point. |
| Buildings | Boxes with fluting, tape and stickers | Everything is visibly made by hand. Nothing is perfectly square. |

Rules: no sharp points on anyone friendly; a thick ink outline on everything (2 px at map scale); one soft highlight and one soft shadow, never a hard gradient.

## 3. Colour

- **Day:** warm cardboard browns, paper cream, and one strong colour per place (spring pink, summer yellow, fall orange, winter blue).
- **Night:** deep blue-violet with warm lantern pools. Lights are the brightest thing on screen. Costume Quest's trick: night is where things look most magical, not most scary.
- **Accents:** pink is for hearts and friendship, gold is for rewards, the red-and-blue airmail stripe is for anything that is a letter, a card or a story.
- **Faces:** blush is always on. A character without blush looks ill.

## 4. What makes a character cute *and* someone you care about

The cute is the hook. The caring comes from the writing. Each named character gets all of these:

1. **A silhouette you can spot from across the map.** Species plus one thing they always carry or wear: Robin's mail bag, Bristle's rake, Ember's sketchbook, Hoot's glasses, Mama Burrow's knitting, Dabble's juice jug, Nib's pencil and tape measure, Clover's flower crown.
2. **A want you could say in one breath.** "To open her juice stand." "For somebody to read his books."
3. **An ordinary fear.** Not dragons. The dark, a sister moving away, being a bother, being too slow.
4. **A quirk that makes the jokes.** Hoot talks in footnotes, Nib says "technically", Dabble rates everything out of ten waves. The joke is always the character, never the situation.
5. **A kind secret.** Something they hid because they cared (Clover waits up for her dad on night shift).
6. **A change you can see.** After their five-heart story something on the map is different: Clover carries her own lantern now.
7. **A gift back.** The player gets something they can wear, so the friendship is visible on Sam too.

Stardew Valley's lesson: people care about villagers they *chose* to spend time with, so friendship is never forced, never timed and never missable. Animal Crossing's lesson: villagers should notice you (they look at you, wave and send a heart bubble once you're friends). Terraria's lesson: every villager should make the world feel more lived-in, with a reason to come back to where they live.

## 5. Writing a heart story

Three short scenes per neighbor, at one, three and five hearts.

| Hearts | Job | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Show the quirk and the want | Something funny and small. End on them liking Sam a bit more. |
| 3 | Show the fear | The ordinary worry under the want. Sam answers with something from their own life (often something Grandma said). Nobody fixes it in one go. |
| 5 | Show the change | They do the brave thing themselves. A friend from another story can turn up. End with the keepsake. |

Keep each scene to 5 to 9 lines. Use the party (Pip, Corry) for one line each at most. Don't use Jules in neighbor stories, because Jules isn't always there.

## 6. World rules the art follows

- **Water is cardboard's enemy.** Puddles, drips and soggy edges are the visual sign of trouble. Dry, crisp cardboard means a place is healed.
- **Everything was made by someone.** Tape, crayon, staples and stickers show up on props, signs and buildings. Grandma Rosie's crayon is the oldest layer.
- **Places change when you help them.** Repairs show up on the map: dry floors, a new bridge with triangles everywhere, lanterns lit.
- **No real money anywhere.** Shops sell for bottle caps you earn by playing, and the art never shows anything that looks like a premium currency.
